The loop

A measurement loop, not a wellness feed.

Hospitals digitise what happens inside their walls. Chronic disease happens outside them. Khai Tracker starts at the other end: a few quiet minutes a day, producing the one thing a waiting room cannot — what actually happened between visits.

The rule. The loop is allowed to be incomplete, but not allowed to lie. A skipped dose stays skipped. A missing reading stays missing. An estimate is labelled as an estimate.

What it does

Built for the space between visits.

No feed, no streak, no guilt. The app does one job: turn everyday observations into a record that survives a language change, a flat battery and a four-minute appointment.

01

Offline, entirely

Every screen works with no network. There is no login wall and no first-launch download. Sync exists, but it is off until a study server is configured.

02

Encrypted per day

AES-256-GCM with a random IV per record and a non-exportable key in the Android Keystore. The participant code and date are bound in as additional authenticated data, so ciphertext cannot be moved between people or days.

03

Bilingual at the record level

Age bands and conditions are stored as stable codes, never display labels. Switching to Hindi does not rewrite the record, and dish names resolve through the library at display time — including after weeks of logging.

04

Real plates, honest ranges

83 regional dishes, measured in katoris, rotis and glasses. Nutrition is a range collapsed to a representative value and always presented as an estimate, because a household portion is not a laboratory measurement.

05

One page, not a data dump

The clinician summary is generated on the phone: completeness, recent readings, flags, medicine status, food frequency and the participant's own note. Designed to be read in under a minute.

06

Interoperable by construction

FHIR R4 with LOINC-coded observations, UCUM units, a real Consent and a Provenance trail. Timestamps carry the day recorded, not an invented clock time. Diet estimates are deliberately kept out of the clinical bundle.

07

Quiet by design

One optional daily reminder. No badge counts, no exact alarms, no re-engagement nags, no advertising identifier. Notification text never contains a reading.

08

Small enough to trust

A 0.9 MB APK built from the framework alone — no AndroidX, no analytics SDK, no transitive dependency surface. The whole build is four Python-driven toolchain steps, reproducible without Gradle or an IDE.

The larger pattern

The hospital loop, rebuilt for a phone.

Closed-loop medication, a longitudinal record, follow-up without travel, and machine reading under human authority. Khai starts with the smallest node that can carry that loop: one participant, one mid-range Android phone, one clinician with four minutes.

Straight answers

Questions worth asking.

If something here reads like marketing rather than a limit, it is a bug. The honest version is more useful.

Is Khai Tracker a medical device?

No. It is a measurement and record-keeping tool. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease, and it never changes a prescription. Decisions about care belong to a qualified clinician.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, completely. Every screen, the clinician summary and all exports are generated on the phone. Sync is optional and does nothing until a study server address is configured by a coordinator.

Where is my data stored?

In the Android app: encrypted in a local SQLite database, with the key held in the phone's non-exportable Android Keystore. The app is excluded from cloud backup and device transfer. In the browser demo: in this browser's localStorage only — no server, no upload, no cookie.

Is the browser demo the real app?

No. The demo is a faithful browser implementation of the same record shape, food library and rules, so you can try the loop without installing anything. The Android app is native Kotlin. The demo is not encrypted and must not be used for real clinical records.

Why isn't it on Google Play?

Because the remaining gates are real: a production keystore and Play App Signing, a device test pass on low-end hardware, real screenshots, and a monitored privacy mailbox. The build is structurally verifiable today; those operational steps are not done.

Can I take my data out?

Yes. Metrics CSV for a spreadsheet, food CSV for a dietitian, a complete JSON copy, a FHIR R4 bundle for a clinical system, and a one-page printable summary. Exports are plaintext by design and go only to the app you choose in the system share sheet.

Who is it for?

Adults in a Khai Foods observational pilot who are living with a long-term condition, often on a shared family phone with intermittent connectivity. The app is designed for that constraint first, not for a flagship device.

Who built it, and is it affiliated with a hospital?

Khai Foods Pvt Ltd, Jamshedpur, under product owner Biswajit Roy Bardhan. The digital-transformation pattern is informed by publicly described hospital practice, including Zhongshan Hospital, but there is no affiliation with, endorsement by, or derivation from Zhongshan, HIMSS or any named health system.

Is there AI in it?

Not in the clinical path in 1.0.0. The progress screen describes regularities in the participant's own short series, with the number of days behind each statement, and suppresses anything resting on fewer than four paired days. Any future assistance must be evaluated, versioned, overridable and audited before it can influence a decision.
